01

The pattern, in one image.

Every analysis you run today uses the same three moves. Master the loop, the prompts get easier.

Quant Analyst
What changed and where?
Crunches the numbers. Finds the pattern. Doesn't theorize yet.
Use first. Establish what's true before deciding what it means.
Thought Partner
What else could explain this?
Slows you down. Generates competing hypotheses. Names what to rule in or out.
Use second. Before any meaningful intervention or budget commitment.
Synthesizer
Tell the right person, the right way.
Packages the analysis. Drafts the memo, the email, the board update.
Use third. Name the audience and the shape. Quality follows.

The dataset: Momentum College.

A fictional hybrid institution in North Carolina, serving both traditional and adult/transfer students. Use it during the session and after.

Momentum College — FY25 & FY26 Admissions & Enrollment

Student-level · 32,500 rows · Two tracks · Two fiscal years

Each row is one prospective student. The dataset covers inquiry through fall enrollment plus first-year persistence outcomes. Linked by student_id across both tracks (Traditional and Adult/Transfer) so you can cut the data any direction.

Planted patterns to find

  1. Traditional Track yield drop. Yield falls ~4 points FY25→FY26. The drop concentrates in out_of_state_merit, where summer melt jumps from ~14% to ~29%. The outreach data shows why.
  2. Adult Track employer-driven stop-out. First-term stop-out climbs in the employer_sponsored segment. Cut by employer, the story is one company (Carolina Health Network) reducing tuition benefits mid-year.
  3. Cross-track equity gap. First-gen Pell-eligible students in STEM programs complete year one at ~48% versus ~81% institution-wide. A longstanding pattern, not a year-over-year change.
03

The prompt library, copy-ready.

Adapt these to your data and your questions. The pattern matters more than the words.

The three-mode scaffold

▸ Quant Analyst
"Look at the [TRACK/SEGMENT] data I loaded. Compare [METRIC] by [DIMENSION] and year. Where in the funnel did the change happen? Show me absolute numbers and percentages. Don't theorize yet."
Why this works: Names the scope, names the metric, asks for both raw and rate, and explicitly tells Claude not to jump ahead.
▸ Thought Partner
"Before I act, give me three competing hypotheses for what's driving this. Rank them by how well the data supports each one, and tell me what evidence would rule each one in or out. Be honest if one should be rejected."
Why this works: "Compete with me" framing. The "rule in or out" language forces specific evidence. Permission to reject one keeps Claude honest.
▸ Synthesizer
"Draft a one-page memo to [AUDIENCE]. Include: the headline finding, the segment driving it, the leading hypothesis with supporting evidence, and two recommended interventions. Match the tone of [REFERENCE: prior briefs, board memos]."
Why this works: Audience + shape = output quality. Without these two anchors, you get a wall of text.

Advanced Quant moves

▸ Funnel Visualization
"Build me a side-by-side visualization of the [SEGMENT/TRACK] funnel for FY25 versus FY26. Show inquiry → application → enrollment, with both absolute numbers and the conversion rate at each stage. Make the year-over-year drops easy to see at a glance, and call out which stage lost the most students."
When to use: Before segment analysis. Establishes the shape of the problem at a 30,000-foot view.
▸ Executive Dashboard
"Stay in the [SEGMENT] segment. Build me an executive dashboard, as a single HTML artifact, showing what's driving the [METRIC] change. Include: a quadrant scatter mapping each [DIMENSION]'s X-change against Y-change with bubble size for volume; a ranked bar chart of the YoY delta; a KPI strip calling out the standout; the underlying data table beneath. Brand it [YOUR COLORS]. Executive-ready. One screen."
When to use: When you need a defensible briefing artifact for a cabinet or board conversation, not just an answer.
▸ Drill-Down Stratification
"Stay in the [SEGMENT] segment. Give me a clean table broken out by [SUB-DIMENSION]. For each one, show FY25 and FY26 counts, the key metric, and the change. Sort by absolute change. Flag any row where the change crosses [THRESHOLD]."
When to use: When a segment moves and you suspect the story is hiding one level deeper. Stratification reveals what aggregation conceals.
04

The Project blueprint, four pieces.

Don't build the perfect Project. Build the smallest useful one this week, then grow it.

i.

System prompt Role · institution · decision style

Tell Claude who it is, what it's for, and how you make decisions. Three to five paragraphs. This is the most important piece — get it right and every prompt gets sharper.

TEMPLATEYou are the [ROLE: e.g., Enrollment Analyst] for [INSTITUTION NAME], a [INSTITUTION TYPE: e.g., regional comprehensive serving 12,000 students across traditional and adult tracks]. Your job is to help [PRIMARY USER: e.g., the VP of Enrollment Management and the President] make defensible decisions about [DECISION DOMAIN: e.g., recruitment strategy, yield interventions, student success investments]. When you analyze our data, work in three modes: • Quant — establish what's true before interpreting • Thought Partner — generate competing hypotheses, rank by evidence, honestly reject weak ones • Synthesizer — package findings for the named audience in the shape requested Our decision-making style is [e.g., evidence-based, with a bias toward small reversible interventions over large irreversible ones; we prefer specific recommendations with owners and dollar costs]. Tone: [e.g., direct, concise, no hedging unless evidence is genuinely mixed]. Confidentiality: [any institutional norms about what stays internal].
ii.

Knowledge files What to load, what to keep out

The Project is only as good as what it knows. Load what helps. Keep out what shouldn't be there.

Safe to load
  • Aggregate funnel data (no PII)
  • De-identified persistence data
  • Strategic plan, board reports, IPEDS pulls
  • Internal frameworks, rubrics, memos
  • Past Claude outputs you want to build on
  • Prior briefs (for voice/format matching)
Do not load
  • Student-level data with identifiers
  • Financial aid awards tied to identities
  • Disciplinary, medical, counseling records
  • Anything FERPA-protected
  • Anything you wouldn't email unencrypted

Rule of thumb: aggregate first, then load. You almost never need student-level data to answer the institutional question.

iii.

Reusable prompts Your saved moves

Save the prompts you'll run over and over. Five to start. Twenty by end of quarter.

Weekly funnel check "Compare this week's inquiry-to-deposit funnel against last year's same week. Flag any segment outside its normal range."
Segment deep-dive "Pull everything we know about [SEGMENT] in the last cycle. Profile, performance, persistence. What stands out?"
Board prep "Draft three slide bullets for the board on [TOPIC]. Headline, root cause, recommended action. Under 20 words each."
Hypothesis stress-test "I'm about to commit to [DECISION]. Give me three reasons this could be wrong, ranked by likelihood."
Memo draft "Draft a one-page memo to [AUDIENCE] on [TOPIC]. Match the format of the last three briefs you've seen."
Peer comparison "Compare our [METRIC] against IPEDS peers in our Carnegie classification. Where do we stand?"
Early warning "Run the persistence model on this term's cohort. Which students would I want a faculty advisor to touch this week?"
Decision pre-mortem "Imagine it's six months from now and [DECISION] failed. Walk me through the most likely failure modes."
iv.

Governance notes Access · review · audit

Write down who can use this Project, what gets reviewed before sharing, and where outputs go.

  • Access: Who can open this Project? Default to you + one delegate this week.
  • Review: What gets human eyes before it leaves the building? Anything going to cabinet, board, or external audiences.
  • Outputs: Where do drafts live? Drafts folder. Where do final versions land? Your sent items, shared drive, or team channel.
  • Audit: Keep a log of which decisions used Claude outputs. You'll want this for Year 2.
05

The agent build recipe, in Cowork.

Turn your analyst Project into an agent that runs on schedule. Twelve minutes, four phases.

1
2 min

Connect the workspace

Define what Cowork can see and touch.

  • ▸ Read access to the data folder
  • ▸ Write access to a /drafts folder
  • ▸ Send access to one email address, gated by owner approval
  • ▸ Reference: last 4 weeks of briefs so the agent learns your voice
2
4 min

Configure the three agents

One Project becomes one agent. Three Projects, three agents, plain-English instructions.

▸ Quant Agent

"Every morning at 8am, read the latest dataset. Compute the seven KPIs we track. Flag anything that has moved more than its threshold. Write the result to /drafts/quant.md."

▸ Thought Partner Agent

"After the Quant Agent finishes, read its output. For each flag, generate one alternative explanation and one thing we'd want to rule out. Write to /drafts/thought-partner.md."

▸ Synthesizer Agent

"After the Thought Partner Agent finishes, read both prior outputs. Draft a one-page cabinet brief in our voice. Match the format of the last four briefs. Write to /drafts/cabinet-brief.md."

3
3 min

Orchestrate the handoff

One sentence in plain English connects all three agents.

ORCHESTRATION"Run Quant. When done, run Thought Partner. When done, run Synthesizer. When done, draft the email and wait for me."
4
2 min

Set trigger, surface, scope

The three decisions that determine when, where, and how much autonomy.

  • ▸ Trigger: every weekday at 8:00am sharp; skip days with no cabinet meeting
  • ▸ Surface: draft email to the cabinet listserv, subject "Cabinet Brief, [date]"
  • ▸ Scope: agent can do everything except hit send; wait for owner approval
  • ▸ Check-in: if not reviewed by 8:45am, send a reminder, never the brief
06

The skills starter pack, build once, reuse forever.

A skill is a packaged capability. Build five, you've encoded the way your institution works.

Skill · 01

Cabinet Brief

Your institutional voice and format, encoded so any agent produces briefs at your cabinet's standard.
What it should contain
  • Section structure (headline, KPIs, finding, action)
  • Word count per section
  • Tone rules (formal, no jargon, present tense)
  • Three example briefs as voice references
Loaded by: Synthesizer Agent
Skill · 02

Yield Diagnostic

The seven KPIs that matter, the segments to cut by, and the thresholds that warrant a flag.
What it should contain
  • The KPI definitions (formula + denominator)
  • Segment taxonomy (your specific cuts)
  • Year-over-year and term-over-term thresholds
  • "What good looks like" reference
Loaded by: Quant Agent
Skill · 03

Hypothesis Stress-Test

The structured pressure-test routine: name three competitors, rank by evidence, name what would falsify each.
What it should contain
  • The three-question framework
  • Evidence ranking rubric
  • Examples of well-rejected hypotheses
  • Common cognitive bias prompts
Loaded by: Thought Partner Agent
Skill · 04 · Universal

FERPA-Safe Analysis

What to aggregate, what to redact, what to never surface. The compliance layer every agent inherits automatically.
What it should contain
  • Field-level redaction rules
  • Minimum aggregation thresholds (n ≥ 10, etc.)
  • What can leave the institution vs. internal only
  • The "would you email this unencrypted?" test
Loaded by: Every agent

The compounding move. Build one skill: every agent on your team can use it. Build five: you've encoded your institution's way of working. Build twenty: new leaders inherit your institutional muscle memory on day one.

07

Governance, the four questions.

An analyst makes a mistake in a chat window. An agent makes a mistake in front of your board. Answer these four at design time, not after.

▸ Data
What can it see?
Aggregate only. No PII. No FERPA-protected records.
e.g., Cabinet Brief Agent reads de-identified funnel and persistence summaries, never individual student files.
▸ Scope
What can it do?
Read, analyze, draft. Never send, post, or commit without approval.
e.g., The Cabinet Brief Agent drafts the email but cannot click send until you review and approve.
▸ Escalation
When does a human get involved?
Anything material gets routed to the owner before it goes out.
e.g., If a flag exceeds 2× normal threshold, the agent pings you on Slack before drafting anything.
▸ Audit
Can we see what it did?
Log every run. Every output. Every decision the agent made.
e.g., All briefs archived to a dated folder; runs that produced no output also logged.

Governance isn't a brake on the agentic move. It's what makes the agentic move possible.
