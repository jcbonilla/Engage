What Q3 gives the network to showcase

  • Pulse supplies the largest volume. Its 24 releases generated 3,999 downloads, 34.1% of Q3. “Understanding the Modern Student Journey, Part 1” reached 230 downloads, 37% above its Q3 median.
  • CMO remains the per-episode leader. Seven releases generated 2,285 downloads, averaging 326.4. It nearly matched Geek’s 2,314 downloads from 21 releases. These are different shows with different audiences, not an efficiency or return comparison.
  • Geek’s numbered episodes show a stronger Q3 result. Their average rose to 122.3 from 109.0 in Q2, a 12.2% observed increase despite being newer. This does not establish a causal improvement, but it is a useful positive signal to investigate.

The editorial decision: protect the content and cadence already producing results, test more specific listener-benefit framing, and evaluate Q4 with a defined measurement date. Podcast creators should approve proposed targets and publishing capacity before treating the plan as a commitment.

The remaining 307 is associated with the change in the observed average. This 93% volume contribution is an arithmetic decomposition, not a causal estimate.

Against YTD: Q3’s 146.4 average is 7.4% below the January–September average of 158.1. YTD includes Q3, so it is context rather than an independent benchmark. The non-overlapping Q1–Q2 baseline is 162.7; Q3 is 10.0% below that older episode cohort. Neither gap proves declining audience demand.

How episode age changes the interpretation

Q1 episodes have had at least 204 days to accumulate downloads. The oldest Q3 episode has had 112. A January episode and a September episode cannot be treated as equal-window outcomes. Dividing lifetime downloads by age would also be misleading because listening does not usually accumulate at a constant rate.

Use first-7-day or first-30-day downloads to measure audience change. For a practical quarter-cohort comparison, capture Q4 on January 21, 2027. Q4 and Q3 both span 92 days, so their snapshot age ranges align at 21–112 days. Differences in actual release dates and format mix still require review.

The Geek rebound is partly a publishing-mix story

Geek formatQ1 episodes / avgQ2 episodes / avgQ3 episodes / avg

Geek event interviews fell from 26 of 39 releases in Q2 to five of 21 in Q3. Numbered episodes improved from 109.0 to 122.3, while event interviews moved from 84.4 to 76.6. Holding Q2’s two-format weights fixed gives Q3 an average of 91.8, versus Q2’s 92.6, an observed 0.8% decrease. That calculation excludes Q3’s two other releases because Q2 had no comparable other category.

What changed: the full show average rose 19.0%, but the mix-adjusted two-format comparison did not. Celebrate the stronger numbered-episode results; do not credit the whole rebound to better content or stronger audience demand.

Content success signatures to carry into Q4

These are patterns and editorial hypotheses, not causal rules. Compare examples with their own show’s Q3 median so CMO’s larger audience does not dominate every content decision.

Repeated format pattern

Numbered episodes consistently draw more downloads than event interviews

Numbered episodes average more downloads than event interviews in all three quarters: 144.6 vs 98.3 in Q1, 109.0 vs 84.4 in Q2, and 122.3 vs 76.6 in Q3.

Use the pattern to test packaging, not to assume events lack value. Guest reach, promotion, timing, duration, and non-download event goals are unknown.

Editorial hypothesis

Strong examples make the listener benefit specific

“Operating System Upgrade” and “Mission-Driven Culture of Innovation” are Geek’s leading Q3 episodes at 177 and 169 downloads, 59% and 52% above its median. Pulse’s student-journey opener is 37% above its median.

Test titles that name a practical problem, audience, or institutional example. These retrospectively selected successes do not prove that wording drove downloads.

Series pattern with age bias

Give later installments a standalone reason to listen

Five of seven identified Pulse series with both an opener and follow-ups have higher opener downloads than the later-installment average. The student-journey opener has 230 versus 164.7 for three follow-ups.

Earlier episodes have more time to accumulate downloads. This is not listener retention. Test distinct episode promises and cross-promotion before shortening series.

Operational goals that make the targets credible

  • 100% measurement coverage: store episode ID, publication date, extraction date, format, promotion activity, and first-7-day and first-30-day downloads for every Q4 release. Retrieve Q3 first-30-day history if the platform supports it.
  • One controlled framing pilot: test two promotional headlines for the same Geek event episode, with comparable recipient groups. Predefine the outcome and minimum worthwhile lift. Without download attribution, call the outcome clicks rather than downloads.
  • One follow-up series pilot: give each installment a specific standalone promise and measure equal-window results. Do not set a retention target from summed episode listeners.

Sources, methods, and limits

Source: Enrollify Q1–Q3 2026 episode dataset, supplied by the user. The user confirmed that counts are lifetime downloads captured October 21, 2026. Q3’s 80 rows exactly match the earlier Q3-only file.

The source contains 279 records, eight shows, no missing values, no duplicate episode keys, no invalid dates, no nonpositive counts, and no listener counts exceeding downloads. Record-level checks do not establish tracking accuracy or export completeness.

Downloads are additive across episode records. Unique listeners are episode-level counts and cannot be summed into unique network audience or retention. No inference of causality, significance, or equal-window growth is made. No predictive model or age-adjustment model was fitted.

Comparison and decomposition definitions

Q3 versus Q2 average = Q3 average ÷ Q2 average − 1. YTD average uses all 279 records, including Q3. The non-overlapping first-half baseline uses 199 episodes published January–June. All show averages are weighted by their own episode counts.

For the Q2-to-Q3 total difference, publishing component = change in episode count × Q2 average. Average component = Q3 episode count × change in average. This exact decomposition assigns the interaction to the average component and does not explain why publishing or observed average changed.

Geek formats use both “Live at” and “Live from” as event interviews. Numbered episodes start with “Episode #”. Other includes two Q3 releases. Fixed-mix comparison uses Q2 weights of 26/39 event interviews and 13/39 numbered episodes, applied to Q3’s format averages.
